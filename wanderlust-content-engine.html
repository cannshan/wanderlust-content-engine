Her caption formula, decoded

Across all 15 captions, the structure repeats almost every time — this is what the dashboard should replicate, not a generic travel-caption template.

HookFirst-person and specific: "Did you know…", "I just spent the day…", or "Have you seen…" — never a generic scene-setter.
Story beatOne unexpected detail (fairy houses on the trail, a jellyfish warning, a waffle folded a specific way) that a stock description wouldn't include.
Practical infoReal logistics woven in — distance from a known landmark, price, or how to book. Occasionally a full itinerary as an emoji bullet list instead of prose (Mont Tremblant: hike → village → gondola → dessert).
CTAEnds on a direct question or an explicit next step ("DM for the link," "Would you try it?!," "Save this for your trip") — drives comments and saves, both weighted heavily.
Location + tagAlmost always a 📍 pin and the venue tagged by handle (@corgibliss, @lonepinebrewing, @reinefjordsjohus) — skipped only a handful of times, usually when no clean handle exists.
HashtagsExactly 5 in 14 of 15 posts: 1–2 broad regional (#visitmaine, #visitnorway) + 1–2 specific/niche (#lofotenislands, #hammondcastle) + 1 community tag (#dogsofinstagram, #castlesofinstagram).
Series framingRecurring bits get numbered — "Maine Lobster Roll Series, stop 9," "Next up in my Oslo series" — makes single posts feel part of a saveable collection.
02

How the algorithm actually grades a trip video

The rules changed enough since 2024 that old advice ("keep it under 15 seconds," "post anything daily") now actively hurts reach.

Watch-through beats everything, including view count

Every video is first shown to a small pool of her own followers. If that pool watches to the end and engages fast, TikTok expands the audience — if they drop early, the video stalls no matter how good the idea was. A rewatch (one person watching three times) counts for more than three people watching once. (Socialync, HeyOrca)

The hook is the whole pitch, not the intro

The spoken line, the first visual frame, and the on-screen caption all have to land inside 1–3 seconds — a curiosity gap, a specific number, or a contrarian claim, not a warm-up. The three formulas that consistently outperform across niches right now are the contrarian claim, the mistake warning, and the list tease. (Kineclip)

Length should match the content, not a magic number

Entertainment clips peak at 15–30s, educational content at 30–60s, and storytelling at 60–90s. Cinematic travel content aimed at adults tends to reward the longer end — the old "shorter always wins" rule doesn't hold anymore. (Quso)

Sound: trending helps a little, original is rewarded more now

A trending sound gives a small distribution boost when it genuinely fits — forced use doesn't help. TikTok rebalanced its 2026 ranking to favor original audio, which also happens to suit a brand built on someone's own voice. (Miraflow)

Travel has its own pattern worth stealing

Specific-number cost reveals ("stayed in a 5-star hotel for $80/night") consistently outperform vague luxury content — the gap between the expectation and the real number is the hook itself. And because TikTok surfaces destination content by search intent, videos about a place tend to get discovered for weeks before people actually travel there — a seasonal content calendar compounds this. (Little Wave Media)

03

Where to actually find "viral" — not guess it

Two free tools, both built by TikTok itself, solve the exact problem she described: not knowing which tags or topics are actually working right now.

TikTok Creative Center (ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter) — no login required. Shows trending hashtags and sounds with view counts, growth rate, and related examples, refreshed every 24–48 hours. This is the single best free source of "what's actually trending right now" data. (Creatify)

Creator Search Insights → Content Gap (in the TikTok app, under Settings) — this is the one most creators never find. It flags searches with high demand and low supply: topics people are actively typing into TikTok search with almost no good videos answering them. Filling a content gap gets rewarded with outsized reach because there's no competition for it. (Hook Studio)

Neither tool has a public API — TikTok doesn't let outside apps pull this data automatically, and scraping it would violate their terms. So the realistic workflow is: she spends two minutes a week skimming both, and the dashboard below takes whatever she found (or nothing at all) and turns it into a usable script.

04

One trip is not one video

This is the real lever for a couple who already travels to film. The same footage from one hike or one vineyard afternoon splits cleanly into several different videos, each built around a different hook — not the same clip re-posted.

Creators who batch-film a single outing typically pull 5–10 short videos out of one session instead of one — what changes between them isn't the footage, it's the framing. (Cutback, PostQuick)

Cost reveal

"$80 for this view"

The exact price, meal cost, or entry fee — travel's single best-performing hook pattern, because the gap between expectation and the real number does the work.

Mistake warning

"We almost skipped this"

What you nearly missed or got wrong — parking, timing, the trail you almost didn't take.

List tease

"3 things to know before you go"

Practical, saveable — logistics content earns the highest save-rate of any travel format.

POV / story

"POV: you found the place"

The cinematic, longer-form cut for storytelling — same clips, slower pacing, 60–90s.

One production notes tip worth adopting on-site: grab a few extra seconds of raw footage before and after every shot. That buffer makes it much easier to re-cut the same clip for a second or third angle later without a reshoot. (PostQuick)

05

What to automate, what to keep human

"Automate video creation" covers a wide range — some of it saves real time safely, some of it is exactly what makes faceless AI channels feel hollow and get penalized.

Automate

  • Description written in her established voice/structure
  • Hashtag sets matched to her proven broad + niche + community ratio
  • Checking a caption draft against current trending tags/sounds
  • Suggested location + business tags to include
  • Flagging when a video fits a repeatable pattern (animal content, pop-culture tie-in) worth leaning into

AI-assist, she decides

  • Which trend is actually on-brand
  • Final take / voice while filming
  • Descript's Underlord first-pass edit — she reviews every cut
  • Which of 3 thumbnail concepts to use

Never automate

  • Posting without her review
  • Bought views, followers, or engagement
  • Fully AI-voiced/AI-faced "faceless" videos
  • Any tool promising guaranteed virality
06

The scammy version — and how to spot it

The YouTube/TikTok "faceless automation" course industry is exactly the noise you flagged. The pattern repeats enough to be a checklist.

✕Guaranteed income promises — "$5,000 in 30 days" is a marketing hook, not a plan. (AI Funnel Insider)
✕Suspicious reviews — one course had 91% five-star ratings, but 87% of those accounts had posted only one review ever. (source above)
✕"Why would they sell the secret?" — if a guru's automation actually printed money reliably, they'd run more channels, not sell courses about it.
✕Fully AI-generated videos underperform — they lack the human connection that builds loyalty, and 100% automated channels typically see near-zero real engagement. (Medium)
✕Broken "auto-post" promises — many paid tools simply fail to post; TikTok requires a real app-review process (2–6 weeks) before any third party can publish to an account, so a $30/month tool claiming instant auto-posting is usually either not really posting for you or violating TikTok's own terms. (TikTok for Developers)

The plan below avoids every one of these because she stays the face, the voice, and the final editor on every video — the dashboard only removes the blank-page problem.

07

Her new workflow

The idea, the trip, and the filming don't change. One step gets replaced: the 15 minutes of guessing at hashtags and staring at the caption box.

Today

  1. Idea + location already decided, footage already shot
  2. Record audio, edit in Descript
  3. Stare at the caption box, write it from scratch
  4. Guess at 5 hashtags, hope they're the right mix
  5. Post, wait, see what sticks
→

With the engine

  1. Idea + location already decided, footage already shot
  2. Record audio, edit in Descript
  3. Enter idea + location + any story beats (1 minute)
  4. Get a description in her voice + a 5-tag set (broad / niche / community), checked against what's trending right now
  5. Tweak, post, schedule via TikTok Studio or Metricool

How it's actually built: a small web form on top of the Claude API (Sonnet), prompted with her real caption formula plus 15–20 of her best examples so the output sounds like her by default — not generic travel-creator copy.
